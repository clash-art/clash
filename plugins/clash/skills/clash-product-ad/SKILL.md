# Product advertising

Deliver the requested image, shot, or edit. A hero image does not require a
complete campaign. In a Clash Project, use the base `clash` skill for execution
and Asset/Revision lineage; follow the user's existing model, budget, and scope.

## Message and evidence

Choose the audience, intended use, one main benefit, and the evidence that can
actually show it. Separate supplied product facts from proposed ad copy. Do not
turn an attractive visual analogy into an unsupported performance claim.
Treat a reference ad as source material for pacing, framing, or structure;
preserve the user's product, branding, and intended message in the new work.

## Product lock and coverage

Inspect the selected product references for silhouette, proportions, controls,
seams, finish, artwork, and state. Use `clash-multiview-consistency` when a needed
view is missing; do not invent an unseen connector or logo. Use
`clash-reference-composition` for a new environment, layout, or hero frame.

Give each shot a purpose: recognition, material detail, demonstration, evidence,
or call to action. A demonstration must show a legible cause and result. Keep
hands, hinges, openings, and assembly state continuous; use `clash-shot-continuity`
when joining action coverage. Favor visible benefit over unrelated spectacle.

```text
Create [image/shot/short edit] communicating [one supported benefit] to [audience].
Use selected product reference [actual input] and preserve [observed details].
Show [specific action, material detail, or evidence] through [camera and framing].
The product begins in [state] and ends in [state].
Use [light, palette, setting] to support [message].
Visible copy and artwork: [exact supplied or approved draft text].
Keep [identity constraints]; vary only [permitted presentation changes].
```

For an edit, derive pacing from the message and requested placement. Let the
benefit register before the next cut. Keep text readable and framing viable in
the intended crop. Use Timeline/finishing skills only when an edit is requested.

## Produce the moving shots

When the brief asks for generated advertising video, carry preparation through
to actual video takes. A Director Stage can establish the product's placement,
camera, lighting intent, and shot progression. Use `clash-director-production`
for its capture-to-video handoff: inspect the captured frame, refine unfinished
appearance with an image model when needed, then bind the selected frame to a
supported video-model input through Clash. Keep product identity references
through each supported step. Discover available reference roles and durations
from the live model contract rather than assuming every model supports both
first and last frames.

Translate each shot's purpose into visible motion, such as a controlled detail
reveal or a readable demonstration. Inspect the generated clip for shape drift,
changing controls or artwork, material flicker, and broken contact. Select and
trim successful video takes into the real Timeline, add the requested copy and
sound, then render and review the complete ad. Retain Stage captures and any
refined keyframes as intermediate references with their exact Asset identities.
Three attractive stills or a timed slideshow do not fulfill a request for
video-model-generated advertising.

## Acceptance

Inspect actual output for product match, believable interaction, factual copy,
readable branding, and a clear message. Review both the first impression and
the full demonstration. For product heroes, inspect silhouette separation and
surface detail at the delivery size: a dark palette still needs readable form,
and gloss, metal, or soft-touch finish should respond differently to light.
Check contact shadows and support geometry for floating or intersecting parts.
Judge the product's prominence and copy space together in the actual crop.
Repair the failing shot or region while retaining
selected references. Return selected project outputs and the editable Timeline
when requested; report unverified identity, copy, or performance explicitly.
