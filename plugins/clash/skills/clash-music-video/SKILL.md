# Music and rhythm editing

Shape the requested visual sequence around the selected audio. Use the base
`clash` skill for Project operations and the Timeline/finishing skills when
editing. Use existing music unless music generation is part of the user's task.

## Read the music

Listen to the actual recording. Identify phrases, sections, accents, changes of
density, and meaningful lyric cues. Preserve the audio Asset and exact source
range. A supplied beat map is evidence to check against playback, not permission
to infer the entire performance from a BPM value.

Define a visual idea and an arc across the musical phrases. Repetition can build
a motif; vary scale, blocking, motion, color, or intensity to show development.
Do not cut at every detected beat by default or pad the sequence with identical
loops to fill the requested duration.

```text
Use [selected audio and source range].
Phrase [time range] changes from [musical state] to [musical state].
Visual motif: [subject, motion, or scene relationship].
At [verified cue], [cut/action/pose/light/graphic event] lands.
Between cues, [continuous movement or intentional held image].
Develop the next phrase through [specific variation or escalation].
Preserve [subject identity and geography] across related coverage.
```

## Build phrase by phrase

Use the chosen audio as the timing anchor. Account for anticipation, impact,
settle, and recovery when choreographing motion; a single pose on a beat does
not establish musical movement. Decide whether a shot crosses a bar or phrase
boundary on purpose. Retain handles for precise placement and transitions.

For lyrics, verify words against the recording and time readable units to the
actual delivery. For recurring characters or objects, reuse selected references
and the continuity skill. Use a motion-graphics skill for authored animation
only when that medium fits the requested result.

## Acceptance

Watch with sound at normal speed, then inspect critical sync points. Check the
phrase arc, cue timing, intentional variation, identity, lyric accuracy, and
the ending. Verify that the final rendered audio and visual duration agree.
Beat timestamps and still images alone cannot validate audiovisual rhythm.

Deliver the requested media and editable Timeline with selected ranges; leave
rhythm/performance unverified if actual audio playback cannot be inspected.
