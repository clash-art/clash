# Narrative scenes

Make the requested scene or beat read as an event with a meaningful change.
This skill may end at a script, storyboard, Stage capture, or short sequence.
Use `clash-film-production` only for a full production; use the base `clash`
skill for Project operations and the appropriate Stage/Timeline skill as needed.

## Dramatic action

Identify who wants what, what resists them, what they do, and what changes.
Translate abstract mood into an observable decision, action, or reaction.
Dialogue should pursue an intention and respond to the moment; avoid having
every character explain the theme. Silent behavior can carry the turn.

Choose scene geography and consequential props. A setup should make the later
action legible without explaining away the surprise. End on the result of the
beat, not on filler added to meet duration. A requested time is a production
constraint whose plausibility must be checked through performance.

```text
In [place/time], [subject] wants [immediate objective].
[Obstacle or conflicting intention] forces [specific action/choice].
Show [performance behavior] and [other subject's response].
The beat changes from [initial state] to [new situation or emotional state].
Stage [positions, relevant props, entrances, gaze, and movement].
Frame [what the audience needs to discover at this moment].
Dialogue/silence: [playable delivery and necessary words].
```

## Coverage and continuity

Choose cameras for the information and performance they reveal. Establish
geography, show consequential action, and preserve a usable reaction. Additional
angles need a purpose; camera movement alone does not create drama.

Use selected subject/set references when rendering new views. Reuse multiview
and reference-composition skills for those bounded problems. Track hand, prop,
costume, action phase, and positions across cuts with `clash-shot-continuity`.
For an intentional ellipsis, make the change readable rather than accidentally
resetting an ongoing action.

## Acceptance

Inspect the requested output for readable intention, cause, reaction, and change.
For a still or Stage sequence, evaluate the supported visual dimension without
claiming acting or audio has been validated. For video, watch actual timing,
physical interaction, performance, dialogue, and transitions. A screenplay's
summed seconds do not establish a playable scene or completed film.

Keep previous versions and selected references when repairing a beat. Report
the actual delivered medium, duration where applicable, and remaining defects.
