# Rehearse and produce a film scene

Read before planning connected coverage. Define the scene's dramatic change:
where the characters begin, what each wants, how they act and listen, and what
has changed by the end. Give each shot a reason to exist within that event.

## Choose coverage for the scene

Use a shared view when geography or joint action needs to read, closer views
for changes of thought, and inserts or reactions that contribute information.
Avoid requiring the same shot-size list for every scene. Keep a concise shot
record: narrative purpose, camera/blocking, selected cast/set variants, start
and end state, performance/sound, intended cut, and selected take/range.

The atomic skills own the detailed work:

- Missing subject/set views: `clash-multiview-consistency`.
- Per-camera keyframes and reference roles: `clash-reference-composition`.
- Action phase, geography, gaze, sound, and edit joins: `clash-shot-continuity`.

Track costume, carried items, and prop states across the film. Keep a selected
voice/performance reference for recurring speakers when the route supports it.
Only compose or repair what the planned scene needs.

## Direct manageable performances

Give a generated shot a manageable action and emotional change. Include
anticipation, listening, and reaction when the performance calls for them.
If coverage exceeds a model's duration, divide it at meaningful action or
dialogue boundaries; update the cut plan rather than padding with repeats.

Establish line order, pauses, interruptions, reactions, and room tone across
the scene. Choose a supported audio workflow: native audiovisual generation,
or separate recorded speech and a supported synchronization process.
A voice reference is a real input, not just a timbre adjective.

Inspect actual spoken words, acting, articulation, and audio joins through
`clash-shot-continuity`. Do not turn every important line into an off-screen
line merely to avoid solving the visible performance. Let recorded delivery
inform cut lengths and caption timing. Transcription checks words and timing;
it does not assess acting or voice identity on its own.

## Prove a connected sequence before scaling

Generate and assemble enough coverage to test a real exchange with cuts,
shared action, and sound. Unrelated technical tests can reveal model limits
but cannot establish scene continuity. Use accepted rehearsal takes in the
final cut when they serve it.

Review the scene, correct the failed assumptions, then expand in manageable
scene batches. Preserve accepted references and takes rather than rebuilding
them to match a changed prompt. Keep unresolved choices visible in production
notes so a resumed session can continue from actual selections.

Use [review-and-repair.md](review-and-repair.md) for the film-level acceptance
and delivery boundary.
